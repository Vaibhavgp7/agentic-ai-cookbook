Pattern 5 of 5. Overview: 09-more-multi-agent-patterns.html. This lab builds on the supervisor lab (07-supervisor-pattern.html). It adds a second level of leads.

1. Why this lab exists

The supervisor lab had one center and four workers. That works when one prompt can hold every worker’s job. Once the team grows to two departments, each with its own specialists, one router has to know everything about payments and everything about fraud. It also tends to call a fraud specialist before anyone has checked whether the payment message was even sent.

Hierarchical routing splits the choice. The top lead chooses a department. The department lead chooses its own specialist. Specialists report back to their own lead, and only the lead talks to the top.

What you will build

  1. incident_lead: chooses payments_lead, fraud_lead, care_desk, or FINISH.
  2. payments_lead: chooses upi_rail, settlement, or RETURN.
  3. fraud_lead: chooses mule_analyst, victim_analyst, or RETURN.
  4. Five specialist nodes that read only their own data and always report to their own lead.

This is still one StateGraph. You do not need a nested subgraph to get two levels.

2. The business story

The incident note at 10:05

“Since 09:40, money has left customers’ accounts on UPI, several shops say they did not receive it, and a group of new accounts each received the same amount within one minute.”

  • incident_lead reads the note and chooses a department. It does not check the UPI system or the accounts itself.
  • payments_lead chooses a specialist: upi_rail (the path the payment message takes) or settlement (the later step that moves the money to the shop’s bank). The specialist reports to the payments lead. The lead may call the other specialist, or go back to the incident lead.
  • fraud_lead does the same with mule_analyst (looks for accounts used to pass stolen money along) and victim_analyst (looks for the customers who lost money).
  • care_desk sits directly under the incident lead. It drafts what customer support should tell people who call, then goes back to the incident lead.
  • When a department comes back, incident_lead reads the new finding and decides whether another department is still needed. It finishes only when the note says what broke, who was hit, and what support should say.

Words you need

UPI rail
The path a UPI payment message takes between the customer’s bank, NPCI, and the shop’s bank.
Settlement
The later step where banks actually move the money to the shop’s bank in a batch file.
Deemed success
The customer’s account was debited, but the shop’s bank did not confirm the credit in time. The payment is pending until it is credited or reversed.
Mule account
An account opened so stolen money can pass through it.
RETURN
What a department lead chooses when its team is done. It maps to the node incident_lead.
hop_count
How many times the top lead has chosen a department. A small maximum stops it bouncing forever.

3. Why an agent has to do this

  • The opening note fits two stories at once: money is stuck on the way to shops, and stolen money is being split across new accounts. Choosing which team should look first means reading the note and judging.
  • Inside payments, “shops were not paid” might be a fault in the message path or in the settlement file. The payments lead uses the specialist’s finding, then decides the team is done.
  • One router for every specialist would need every specialist’s instructions in one prompt. It would also tend to call the mule analyst before anyone has checked whether the “payment successful” message was sent at all.

Dotted arrows are choices made by a lead. Solid arrows are specialists reporting back to their own lead. A specialist never calls another department.

  • incident_lead sets next to a department lead, to care_desk, or to FINISH.
  • A department lead sets next to one of its specialists, or to RETURN.
  • A specialist’s arrow is fixed. upi_rail always returns to payments_lead. It cannot jump to fraud.
  • Two levels means separate Literal lists. Each contains only that lead’s own destinations.

8. Three routing schemas, one per lead

The top lead’s Literal does not contain upi_rail. It cannot skip a department lead. The payments lead’s Literal does not contain mule_analyst. It cannot reach into fraud.

Your Task

New cell.

class IncidentRoute(BaseModel):
    reason: str = Field(description="One sentence: what is known, what is still missing, and why this is next.")
    next: Literal["payments_lead", "fraud_lead", "care_desk", "FINISH"]
    final_note: str = Field(
        default="",
        description="Only when next is FINISH: 4-6 sentences saying what broke, who was hit, and what support should say.",
    )

class PaymentsRoute(BaseModel):
    reason: str
    next: Literal["upi_rail", "settlement", "RETURN"]

class FraudRoute(BaseModel):
    reason: str
    next: Literal["mule_analyst", "victim_analyst", "RETURN"]

print("Route schemas ready.")

9. The three lead prompts

Each lead knows only its own team. The incident lead does not know what a certificate on a connector is. It only knows which department to ask.

Your Task

New cell.

INCIDENT_LEAD_PROMPT = """You lead the incident room at Horizon Bank. You do not check systems yourself.
You choose a department, read what it reports, and decide what is still missing.

Departments:
- payments_lead: whether the UPI message path or the settlement file explains unpaid shops.
- fraud_lead: whether new accounts receiving the same amount are mules, and who the victims are.
- care_desk: drafts what support should tell callers. Only useful after the facts are known.
- FINISH: only when the notes say what broke, who was hit, and what support should say.

Order of work:
1. If nobody has checked whether the payment message reached the shops' banks, choose payments_lead first.
   Shops not being paid can be a system fault. Do not assume fraud before that is checked.
2. If payments has reported and nobody has checked the new accounts, choose fraud_lead.
3. If both departments reported and care_desk has not, choose care_desk.
4. If care_desk has reported, choose FINISH and write final_note.
Never open the same department twice unless a later finding contradicts it.
"""

PAYMENTS_LEAD_PROMPT = """You lead the payments team. Your specialists:
- upi_rail: the switch log. Did the payment message reach the shop's bank?
- settlement: the settlement file. Will shops be paid, or customers reversed?
- RETURN: give control back to the incident lead.

Choose upi_rail first. After upi_rail reports, choose settlement if shops' money status is unclear.
Choose RETURN when both have reported, or when the incident lead's question is answered.
You cannot see accounts or fraud data. Do not guess about mules.
"""

FRAUD_LEAD_PROMPT = """You lead the fraud team. Your specialists:
- mule_analyst: new accounts that received the same amount.
- victim_analyst: the customers who sent that money.
- RETURN: give control back to the incident lead.

Choose mule_analyst first. After it reports, choose victim_analyst to find who lost money.
Choose RETURN when both have reported.
Use payments' earlier notes to tell whether the fraud is the same event as the shop outage.
"""

def lead_chain(prompt, schema):
    return ChatPromptTemplate.from_messages([
        ("system", prompt),
        MessagesPlaceholder("messages"),
    ]) | llm.with_structured_output(schema)

incident_chain = lead_chain(INCIDENT_LEAD_PROMPT, IncidentRoute)
payments_chain = lead_chain(PAYMENTS_LEAD_PROMPT, PaymentsRoute)
fraud_chain = lead_chain(FRAUD_LEAD_PROMPT, FraudRoute)
print("Lead chains ready.")

10. The lead nodes

The top lead increments hop_count when it opens a department. If hop_count has reached MAX_HOPS, Python forces FINISH, no matter what the model said. Department leads have a smaller guard: once both their specialists have reported, Python forces RETURN.

Your Task

New cell.

DEPARTMENTS = {"payments_lead", "fraud_lead", "care_desk"}

def reported(state, name):
    return any(getattr(m, "name", None) == name for m in state["messages"])

def incident_lead(state):
    decision = incident_chain.invoke({"messages": state["messages"]})
    nxt = decision.next
    if nxt in DEPARTMENTS and state["hop_count"] >= MAX_HOPS:
        print(f"\n[incident_lead] hop limit {MAX_HOPS} reached -> forcing FINISH")
        nxt = "FINISH"
    print(f"\n[incident_lead] -> {nxt}   ({decision.reason})")
    update = {"next": nxt}
    if nxt in DEPARTMENTS:
        update["hop_count"] = state["hop_count"] + 1
    if nxt == "FINISH":
        update["final_note"] = decision.final_note or decision.reason
    return update

def make_department_lead(name, chain, team):
    def node(state):
        decision = chain.invoke({"messages": state["messages"]})
        nxt = decision.next
        if nxt != "RETURN" and all(reported(state, s) for s in team):
            nxt = "RETURN"
        print(f"   [{name}] -> {nxt}   ({decision.reason})")
        return {"next": nxt}
    return node

payments_lead = make_department_lead("payments_lead", payments_chain, ["upi_rail", "settlement"])
fraud_lead = make_department_lead("fraud_lead", fraud_chain, ["mule_analyst", "victim_analyst"])
print("Lead nodes ready.")

11. The specialist nodes

Specialists only add a finding. They do not set next. Their exit is a fixed edge to their own lead. One helper builds all five, each with its own record.

Your Task

New cell.

specialist_prompt = ChatPromptTemplate.from_messages([
    ("system", """You are the {name} specialist. Use only YOUR RECORD and earlier notes.
Write 2 or 3 sentences: what your record shows, and what it means for the incident.
If your record says something is NOT related to another team's finding, say so plainly.
Do not choose who acts next."""),
    MessagesPlaceholder("messages"),
    ("human", "YOUR RECORD:\n{record}"),
])
specialist_chain = specialist_prompt | llm

def make_specialist(name, indent="      "):
    def node(state):
        reply = specialist_chain.invoke({
            "name": name,
            "record": DATA[name],
            "messages": state["messages"],
        })
        text = reply.content
        print(f"{indent}[{name}] {text}")
        return {"messages": [HumanMessage(content=f"{name}: {text}", name=name)]}
    return node

upi_rail = make_specialist("upi_rail")
settlement = make_specialist("settlement")
mule_analyst = make_specialist("mule_analyst")
victim_analyst = make_specialist("victim_analyst")
care_desk = make_specialist("care_desk", indent="   ")
print("Specialists ready.")

12. Wire the graph

Three conditional maps, one per lead. RETURN maps to "incident_lead". Five fixed edges: four specialists to their department lead, and care_desk to the top.

Your Task

New cell.

g = StateGraph(State)
for name, fn in [
    ("incident_lead", incident_lead),
    ("payments_lead", payments_lead),
    ("fraud_lead", fraud_lead),
    ("care_desk", care_desk),
    ("upi_rail", upi_rail),
    ("settlement", settlement),
    ("mule_analyst", mule_analyst),
    ("victim_analyst", victim_analyst),
]:
    g.add_node(name, fn)

g.add_edge(START, "incident_lead")

g.add_conditional_edges("incident_lead", lambda s: s["next"], {
    "payments_lead": "payments_lead",
    "fraud_lead": "fraud_lead",
    "care_desk": "care_desk",
    "FINISH": END,
})
g.add_conditional_edges("payments_lead", lambda s: s["next"], {
    "upi_rail": "upi_rail",
    "settlement": "settlement",
    "RETURN": "incident_lead",
})
g.add_conditional_edges("fraud_lead", lambda s: s["next"], {
    "mule_analyst": "mule_analyst",
    "victim_analyst": "victim_analyst",
    "RETURN": "incident_lead",
})

g.add_edge("upi_rail", "payments_lead")
g.add_edge("settlement", "payments_lead")
g.add_edge("mule_analyst", "fraud_lead")
g.add_edge("victim_analyst", "fraud_lead")
g.add_edge("care_desk", "incident_lead")

graph = g.compile()
print("Graph compiled.")

def run_incident():
    result = graph.invoke(
        {"messages": [HumanMessage(content=INCIDENT, name="incident_note")], "next": "", "hop_count": 0, "final_note": ""},
        config={"recursion_limit": 40},
    )
    path = [m.name for m in result["messages"] if m.name and m.name != "incident_note"]
    print("\n" + "=" * 70)
    print("SPECIALISTS WHO REPORTED:", " -> ".join(path))
    print("DEPARTMENTS OPENED:", result["hop_count"])
    print("\nFINAL NOTE:\n")
    print(result["final_note"])
    return result

result = run_incident()

If the run looks wrong

  • The first department is fraud. The incident lead jumped to the scary words. Re-read its first reason, and check the “order of work” in its prompt.
  • A specialist line is followed by another department’s node. That cannot happen with the fixed edges. If you see it, a specialist edge points at the wrong lead.
  • hop limit reached prints. The top lead opened the same department twice. The guard stopped it. The final note may then be thin. Read which department repeated.
  • The final note blames one event for everything. The victim analyst’s line “None of these payments are in the deemed-success list” is the evidence that they are separate. The incident lead must read the fraud team’s finding together with payments’.

16. Key takeaways

  • Each lead chooses within its own team. Its Literal is its team list.
  • RETURN is just a mapping. It is a string the department lead returns, mapped to the node incident_lead.
  • Specialists do not route. Fixed edges keep each one inside its department.
  • One StateGraph is enough. Nested subgraphs are a later way to package the same idea.
  • Guards belong in Python. hop_count at the top and “both specialists reported” in each department stop a confused model from bouncing.
