Voting Ensemble Pattern Lab: Three Examiners, One Letter of Credit

How to use this guide
  1. Open this page beside a Jupyter notebook named 09d-voting-ensemble.ipynb in the 03-langgraph folder.
  2. Use the .env already in that folder.
  3. Whenever you see Your Task, paste the code into a new notebook cell and run it.
  4. The three examiners print in whatever order they finish. That order is not a bug. They run in one parallel step.

Pattern 4 of 5. Overview: 09-more-multi-agent-patterns.html. This lab reuses the fan-out / fan-in and reducer ideas from 03_parallelization.html.

1. Why this lab exists

Sometimes the right answer is to have several people read the same document before anyone sees the others’ answers. If the second reader sees the first draft, the second opinion is no longer independent.

The graph has no conditional edges at all. Nobody chooses the next node. The three examiners always run, at the same time, and the chair always runs after them. The judgment lives in the votes and in the chair’s note.

What you will build

  1. Three examiners with the same papers and different instructions: strict, commercial, and the buyer’s view.
  2. A votes list with a reducer, so three parallel writes become one list.
  3. A chair that counts the labels in Python, then asks the model to write either a majority note or a split note for the human officer.

2. The business story

The papers on the trade desk

The letter of credit says “100% cotton yarn.” The invoice says “100% cotton yarns, carded.” The bill of lading shortens the port name. Two dates are one day apart.

  • A trade desk (the team that pays sellers in overseas trade) receives papers under a letter of credit (the bank’s promise to pay the seller if the papers match the deal).
  • Three examiners see the same papers at the same time. None of them can see the others’ answers.
  • strict_examiner checks the papers the way a clerk does under UCP 600 (the rulebook banks use for letters of credit). A small difference in wording can be enough to refuse payment.
  • commercial_examiner asks whether a banker who knows this goods trade would treat the two descriptions as the same shipment.
  • applicant_examiner looks at it from the applicant’s side (the buyer who asked the bank to open the letter of credit). Can that buyer fairly refuse to pay, or would a refusal just be an excuse?
  • Each examiner returns one label — complying, discrepant, or waive — and a short reason tied to the actual words.
  • If two or three agree, chair records that label and writes one paragraph on the examiner who disagreed. If all three differ, chair does not pick a winner. It writes a short note that tells the human officer what they disagree about.

Words you need

Letter of credit (LC)
The bank promises to pay the seller if the documents match the terms of the LC.
Bill of lading
The shipping receipt from the carrier. It names the port of loading and the goods.
UCP 600
The international rulebook banks use to examine LC documents.
complying
The papers match the LC. The bank can pay.
discrepant
The papers do not match. The bank may refuse.
waive
There is a mismatch, but the bank can set it aside, usually with the applicant’s agreement.
Reducer
The rule for merging several writes to one state field. Here operator.add joins three one-item lists into one list of three votes.

3. Why an agent has to do this

  • “Yarn” versus “yarns, carded” is a question of language and of the goods. A rule that compares the two strings flags every plural and every extra word, including ones that do not matter.
  • The three answers are judgments. They are useful because the examiners do not see each other’s draft.
  • Counting the votes is a few lines of Python. Writing the note from three different reasons, or explaining a three-way split to an officer, still needs a model.

Every arrow is solid. The three examiners always run in one parallel step. They do not choose the next node. Each one writes a vote into state.

  • Three arrows leave __start__. LangGraph runs those three nodes in the same step. The chair waits until all three have written.
  • Each examiner returns {"votes": [one_vote]}. The reducer joins them into one list of three.
  • Without the reducer, three nodes write the same key in one step, and LangGraph raises InvalidUpdateError. That is the same error you saw in the fan-in lab.
  • The chair counts in Python. Majority: the model writes a note from the winning reasons and mentions the dissent. 1–1–1: the model writes a split note and does not choose.

7. State with a reducer

votes is the field all three examiners write in the same step. It must have operator.add. packet is read-only. decision_note is written once, by the chair.

Your Task

New cell.

class Vote(BaseModel):
    examiner: str
    stance: Literal["complying", "discrepant", "waive"]
    reason: str = Field(description="Two sentences. Quote the exact words from the papers that decide it.")

class State(TypedDict):
    packet: str
    votes: Annotated[list, operator.add]
    decision_note: str

print("Vote and State ready.")

8. Three examiner prompts

The prompts share one instruction block and add one stance each. They do not mention the other examiners’ answers, because those answers do not exist yet when the three nodes start.

Your Task

New cell.

BASE = """You examine trade documents for Horizon Bank under a letter of credit.
You will see only the packet. You cannot see any other examiner.
Return one stance: complying, discrepant, or waive. Quote the words from the papers that decide it.
Do not invent a fact that is not in the packet.
"""

STRICT = BASE + """
Your view: strict document checker under UCP 600.
An extra descriptive word on the invoice that is not in the LC, such as "carded", is a description mismatch.
If any difference could let the bank refuse, your stance is discrepant.
"""

COMMERCIAL = BASE + """
Your view: a banker who knows the cotton yarn trade.
"Carded" is an ordinary process term for cotton yarn and does not describe different goods.
"JNPT Nhava Sheva" is the common short name for Jawaharlal Nehru Port.
If a banker in this trade would treat the papers as the same shipment, your stance is complying.
"""

APPLICANT = BASE + """
Your view: the applicant, the buyer who asked the bank to open this LC.
Ask whether the buyer would be fairly harmed. If the goods are what the buyer ordered and the
differences are wording only, a refusal would just be an excuse.
When there IS a wording difference but the buyer is not harmed, your stance is waive:
the mismatch exists, and the bank can set it aside with the applicant's agreement.
"""

def examiner_chain(system_prompt):
    return ChatPromptTemplate.from_messages([
        ("system", system_prompt),
        ("human", "Packet:\n{packet}"),
    ]) | llm.with_structured_output(Vote)

strict_chain = examiner_chain(STRICT)
commercial_chain = examiner_chain(COMMERCIAL)
applicant_chain = examiner_chain(APPLICANT)
print("Examiner chains ready.")

9. Three examiner nodes

Each node returns a list with one vote. The reducer adds lists together. Set examiner in Python after the model call, so a model typo cannot change who voted.

Your Task

New cell.

def make_examiner(name, chain):
    def node(state):
        vote = chain.invoke({"packet": state["packet"]})
        vote.examiner = name
        print(f"\n[{name}] {vote.stance}\n   {vote.reason}")
        return {"votes": [vote.model_dump()]}
    return node

strict_examiner = make_examiner("strict_examiner", strict_chain)
commercial_examiner = make_examiner("commercial_examiner", commercial_chain)
applicant_examiner = make_examiner("applicant_examiner", applicant_chain)
print("Examiner nodes ready.")

10. The chair

Counting the labels is Python: Counter. The model never decides what the majority is. The model only writes the note. A majority gets a recorded label plus a paragraph on the dissent. A 1–1–1 split gets refer to officer.

Your Task

New cell.

def format_votes(votes):
    return "\n".join(f"- {v['examiner']}: {v['stance']} — {v['reason']}" for v in votes)

majority_prompt = ChatPromptTemplate.from_messages([
    ("system", """You are the chair of the trade desk. The majority stance is already decided in code.
Write a note of 4 to 6 sentences: record the majority stance, summarize the winning reasons,
and write one paragraph on the examiner who disagreed and why their concern is or is not enough.
Do not change the majority stance. Do not add facts outside the packet and the votes."""),
    ("human", "MAJORITY STANCE: {winner}\n\nVOTES:\n{votes}\n\nPACKET:\n{packet}"),
])

split_prompt = ChatPromptTemplate.from_messages([
    ("system", """You are the chair of the trade desk. The three examiners gave three different stances.
Do NOT pick a winner. Write a short note for the human trade officer:
what each examiner believes, which words in the papers they disagree about,
and the one question the officer must answer to decide. Start with: REFER TO OFFICER."""),
    ("human", "VOTES:\n{votes}\n\nPACKET:\n{packet}"),
])

majority_chain = majority_prompt | llm
split_chain = split_prompt | llm

def chair(state):
    votes = state["votes"]
    counts = Counter(v["stance"] for v in votes)
    print("\n[chair] votes received:", len(votes))
    print("[chair] counts:", dict(counts))
    winner, top = counts.most_common(1)[0]
    payload = {"votes": format_votes(votes), "packet": state["packet"]}
    if top >= 2:
        print(f"[chair] majority: {winner} ({top} of {len(votes)})")
        note = majority_chain.invoke({**payload, "winner": winner}).content
        note = f"RECORDED: {winner} ({top} of {len(votes)})\n\n{note}"
    else:
        print("[chair] three-way split: refer to officer")
        note = split_chain.invoke(payload).content
    return {"decision_note": note}

print("Chair ready.")
Why the chair checks len(votes). The chair runs once, after all three examiners. If you see votes received: 1, a fan-in edge is missing, or the reducer is missing and only one write survived.

What you should see

ExaminerExpected stanceWhy
strict_examinerdiscrepant“carded” is on the invoice and not in the LC, and the port name is shortened.

With those three labels the chair prints three-way split: refer to officer, and the note starts with REFER TO OFFICER. It should name the disputed words, usually “carded,” and ask the officer one question.

If two examiners agree, you will see RECORDED: ... and a paragraph on the third. That is also a correct run of the pattern. The chair counted in Python, and the model wrote the note for that count.

If the run looks wrong

  • InvalidUpdateError. votes is a plain list in State. Add Annotated[list, operator.add].
  • votes received: 1 or 2. A fan-in edge to chair is missing. All three examiners need an edge to the chair.
  • The chair says “REFER TO OFFICER” on a 2–1 vote. The top >= 2 test is wrong, or counts was built from reason instead of stance.
  • An examiner’s reason mentions another examiner. The prompt leaked the other answers, or you ran them in sequence. In this graph they cannot see each other.

14. When the number of examiners can change

Three fixed nodes are fine when the panel is always the same three roles. If the panel size changes from case to case, replace the three nodes with one examiner node and Send (the tool from the parallel labs that starts one copy of a node per item). Pass each examiner’s role in that copy. The chair stays the node that waits for all of them, and votes keeps the same reducer.

15. Key takeaways

  • No node chooses a route. The graph is fixed. The variation is in the three votes.
  • Independence comes from parallel start. All three examiners start from the same state, before any vote is written.
  • The reducer is required. Three writes to votes in one step need operator.add.
  • Count in code, explain with the model. Counter decides majority or split. The model writes what a person can read.
  • A three-way split is a result. The chair hands the disagreement to a human. It does not force a winner.
