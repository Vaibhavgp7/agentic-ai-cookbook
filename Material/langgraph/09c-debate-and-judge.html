Pattern 3 of 5. Overview: 09-more-multi-agent-patterns.html.

1. Why this lab exists

Some decisions are not a pass or a fail. Both sides have real evidence, and the useful answer is a launch with conditions. A score that averages the two sides cannot write that note. It also cannot force the second round to answer what the other side just said.

Bull and bear do not choose the next node. If they could, one of them would try to skip the other or skip the judge. A small Python node, turn_gate, counts the rounds and owns the loop.

What you will build

  1. A bull that argues for offering a small credit line on the UPI pay screen.
  2. A bear that argues against offering it to everyone.
  3. A turn gate that is plain Python. It sends the case back to the bull once, then to the judge.
  4. A judge that may use only claims already in the debate, and must end with conditions.

2. The business story

The question in front of the product committee

Should Horizon Bank show a pre-approved credit line when an existing customer pays with UPI?

  • The product team wants a credit line (a pre-approved borrowing limit) on the pay screen. Fraud, customer complaints, and the bank’s own money that would cover losses all matter. Both sides have real evidence.
  • bull argues for the launch: other apps already offer it, customers who receive salary in this bank have a low loss rate, and a small cap (a limit on one payment) keeps the risk down.
  • bear argues against offering it to everyone: mule accounts (accounts opened to pass stolen money along) can still show a salary credit, complaints jump when an automatic payment fails, and the loss numbers cover only six months.
  • Each side speaks twice. The second turn must answer the other side. It must not repeat the opening.
  • judge reads the whole exchange and writes the decision: launch only for salary customers, cap the amount, and look at the loss numbers again after three months. It must say which of the bear’s points it accepted and which of the bull’s points it set aside.

Words you need

Credit line
A limit the customer can borrow up to, already approved, without a new application at the moment of payment.
Cap
The largest amount one UPI payment may put on that credit line. In this brief it is ₹5,000.
Mule account
An account opened so stolen money can pass through it. A salary credit on that account does not make it safe.
Round
One bull speech plus the bear speech that follows it. This lab runs two rounds.
Turn gate
The Python node that increments round and chooses bull or judge. It does not argue.

3. Why an agent has to do this

  • Both sides can be right at the same time. The answer is a decision with conditions, not a pass or fail score.
  • The second round helps only if each side actually replies to what the other side just said.
  • The judge has to tie each condition to a specific argument. A form that averages two scores cannot write that note.

bull, bear, and judge are model calls. turn_gate is plain Python: it only counts rounds. The only dotted arrows leave the turn gate.

  • One round always goes bull, then bear, then turn_gate. Those are solid arrows. Neither speaker can skip the other.
  • turn_gate adds 1 to round. While the new value is less than 2, it sends the case back to bull. Bull can now see what bear just said.
  • After the second round it sends the case to judge. A solid arrow then ends the graph.
  • Two rounds is a choice for this lesson. You can also stop early if the bear says it has nothing new. Keep a hard stop either way. This lab uses the hard stop only.

7. State

messages uses operator.add. Each speech is one AIMessage with name="bull" or name="bear", so the other side can see who spoke. round is a plain integer. The gate replaces it. Do not put a reducer on round, or the count will be added twice.

8. Bull and bear

The prompts are different. Each side is told which evidence it may use, and both are told: on the second turn, quote the other side before you add a point. Do not repeat your opening.

Your Task

New cell.

BULL_PROMPT = """You are the bull on a product committee at Horizon Bank.
Argue FOR showing the credit line on the UPI pay screen.

Use only the QUESTION, the BRIEF, and the debate so far.
You may use the bull evidence. You may answer the bear, but you may not invent numbers.

If you have not spoken yet, give a short opening: three points, under 120 words.
If you have spoken already, this is your second turn:
- Quote one sentence from the latest bear message.
- Answer that sentence.
- Do not repeat your opening points.
"""

BEAR_PROMPT = """You are the bear on a product committee at Horizon Bank.
Argue AGAINST offering the credit line to every existing customer.

Use only the QUESTION, the BRIEF, and the debate so far.
You may use the bear evidence. You may answer the bull, but you may not invent numbers.

If you have not spoken yet, give a short opening: three points, under 120 words.
If you have spoken already, this is your second turn:
- Quote one sentence from the latest bull message.
- Answer that sentence.
- Do not repeat your opening points.
"""

def speaker_chain(system_prompt):
    prompt = ChatPromptTemplate.from_messages([
        ("system", system_prompt),
        ("human", "QUESTION:\n{question}\n\nBRIEF:\n{brief}"),
        MessagesPlaceholder("messages"),
    ])
    return prompt | llm

bull_chain = speaker_chain(BULL_PROMPT)
bear_chain = speaker_chain(BEAR_PROMPT)

def bull(state):
    reply = bull_chain.invoke(state)
    text = reply.content if isinstance(reply.content, str) else str(reply.content)
    print(f"\n[bull] round so far {state['round']}\n{text}\n")
    return {"messages": [AIMessage(content=text, name="bull")]}

def bear(state):
    reply = bear_chain.invoke(state)
    text = reply.content if isinstance(reply.content, str) else str(reply.content)
    print(f"\n[bear] round so far {state['round']}\n{text}\n")
    return {"messages": [AIMessage(content=text, name="bear")]}

print("Speakers ready.")

9. The turn gate

This node does not call the model. It adds 1 to round. The value you store is the number of completed rounds. Start at 0. After the first pair, the value is 1, and 1 is still less than 2, so the next node is bull. After the second pair, the value is 2, which is not less than 2, so the next node is judge.

Your Task

New cell. Trace it on paper with round starting at 0 before you run the graph.

def turn_gate(state):
    nxt = state["round"] + 1
    choice = "another_round" if nxt < 2 else "enough"
    print(f"\n[turn_gate] completed rounds = {nxt} -> {choice}")
    return {"round": nxt, "next": choice}

print("Turn gate ready.")
Why the speakers do not set next. Bull and bear return only a message. If the bull node could return next="judge", it could skip the bear. The solid edges bull → bear → turn_gate make that impossible. Only the gate has conditional edges.

10. The judge

The judge is told not to add a new fact. It may use only claims already in the debate. It must name one bear point it accepts and one bull point it sets aside. The decision has to include the conditions, not a bare yes or no.

Your Task

New cell.

class Ruling(BaseModel):
    accepted_from_bear: str = Field(description="One bear point you accept, in the bear's words as closely as you can.")
    set_aside_from_bull: str = Field(description="One bull point you do not treat as enough, and why.")
    conditions: list[str] = Field(description="The conditions on any launch. At least two.")
    decision: str = Field(description="The ruling in 4 to 6 sentences. No new numbers.")

judge_prompt = ChatPromptTemplate.from_messages([
    ("system", """You are the judge. You do not add a new fact and you do not invent a number.
Use only claims that already appear in the debate messages.
If a speaker used a figure that is not in the brief, ignore that figure.

Write a decision with conditions. A reasonable reading of this brief is:
launch only for customers who receive salary in this bank, keep the ₹5,000 cap,
and review the loss numbers after three more months.
You may differ if the debate supports it, but you must say which bear point you accepted
and which bull point you set aside.
Do not end with only "yes" or only "no".
"""),
    ("human", "QUESTION:\n{question}\n\nBRIEF:\n{brief}"),
    MessagesPlaceholder("messages"),
])
judge_chain = judge_prompt | llm.with_structured_output(Ruling)

def judge(state):
    ruling = judge_chain.invoke(state)
    text = (
        f"ACCEPTED FROM BEAR: {ruling.accepted_from_bear}\n"
        f"SET ASIDE FROM BULL: {ruling.set_aside_from_bull}\n"
        f"CONDITIONS: {'; '.join(ruling.conditions)}\n\n"
        f"{ruling.decision}"
    )
    print("\n[judge]\n" + text)
    return {
        "decision": text,
        "messages": [AIMessage(content=text, name="judge")],
    }

print("Judge ready.")

11. Wire the graph

Your Task

New cell. Confirm there is no conditional edge on bull or bear.

g = StateGraph(State)
g.add_node("bull", bull)
g.add_node("bear", bear)
g.add_node("turn_gate", turn_gate)
g.add_node("judge", judge)

g.add_edge(START, "bull")
g.add_edge("bull", "bear")
g.add_edge("bear", "turn_gate")
g.add_conditional_edges("turn_gate", lambda state: state["next"], {
    "another_round": "bull",
    "enough": "judge",
})
g.add_edge("judge", END)

graph = g.compile()
print("Graph compiled.")

13. Run the debate

Your Task

New cell. You should see two [bull] blocks and two [bear] blocks before [judge].

def run_debate():
    result = graph.invoke(
        {
            "question": QUESTION,
            "brief": BRIEF,
            "messages": [HumanMessage(content="Open the debate.", name="chair")],
            "round": 0,
            "next": "",
            "decision": "",
        },
        config={"recursion_limit": 15},
    )
    print("\n" + "=" * 70)
    print("COMPLETED ROUNDS:", result["round"])
    names = [m.name for m in result["messages"] if m.name in ("bull", "bear", "judge")]
    print("SPEAKING ORDER:", " -> ".join(names))
    return result

result = run_debate()

Check the judge’s text against the brief:

  • It accepts something the bear actually said (mule accounts with a salary credit, complaints when autopay fails, or the six-month study).
  • It sets aside something the bull said, usually “other apps already do this” or the 0.4 percent figure treated as if it were a full year.
  • The conditions include who may see the line (salary customers), the ₹5,000 cap, and a later look at the loss numbers.
  • It does not introduce a new percentage.

If the run looks wrong

  • Only one round. The gate went straight to judge. Check that the initial state sets round to 0, and that the comparison is nxt < 2, not nxt < 1.
  • Three rounds. The comparison is too loose. After the increment, nxt < 2 is true only when nxt is 1, so the gate loops back once and you get two rounds. nxt < 3 loops back twice and you get three rounds.
  • The second bull speech repeats the opening. The model ignored the second-turn rule. The graph is still correct. In the exercise section you will add a check.
  • The judge cites a figure you cannot find in the brief or the speeches. That breaks the judge prompt. Quote the sentence back in a follow-up cell and ask which message it came from. If it came from nowhere, the judge added a fact.

14. Key takeaways

  • The arguers do not own the loop. Solid edges force bull then bear. The gate decides whether the round is over.
  • A second turn is only useful if it is a reply. The prompt has to say “quote the other side.” The graph cannot check English by itself.
  • The judge is a different job from a third debater. It does not add evidence. It ties conditions to points already made.
  • The hard stop is the gate’s comparison, not a hope that the model will get tired. Two rounds is a lesson choice. A production gate can also stop early, and it still needs a maximum.
  • Shared messages are the debate. operator.add is what lets the second bull see the first bear.
